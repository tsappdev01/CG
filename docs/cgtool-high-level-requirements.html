CGTOOL high-level requirements

Ten requirements covering the Corporate Governance Tool, each with a wireframe of the screen that satisfies it. Derived from the 55-story backlog.

Dubai Investments PJSC · October 2026 · for vendor review

The wireframes are sketches, not the design. CGTOOL is built and its interface is finished. These drawings exist only to show what each screen has to carry — which fields, which states, which decisions — so a requirement can be read without opening the application. Nothing about their layout, spacing or styling describes the product.

Identity and personal records

HLR-1

Access is granted per module and proven per person

The system must authenticate through the corporate directory rather than its own credential store, and must grant each person access to each of the four modules independently. A person signed in but not yet linked to a staff record must be told so by name of the screen that fixes it, rather than shown an empty application. Every grant and revocation must be attributable.

HLR-2

Each person keeps one standing register of their related parties

The system must hold each person's relatives and the companies they or their relatives have an interest in as a standing record, entered once and reused by every declaration period. It must refuse duplicates by showing the existing record, track document expiry rather than presenting lapsed documents as current, and keep every addition, change and deletion attributable.

Declarations

HLR-3

Declaration periods are opened deliberately and their recipients are provable

The system must let an administrator open a declaration period for a named quarter and a chosen population, send the notification, remind those still outstanding, and stop reminding anyone who has submitted. It must be able to show, for any person and any period, exactly what was sent to them and when — a claim of "I never received it" has to be answerable from the system rather than from a mailbox.

HLR-4

A declaration is captured in steps, saved as a draft, and submitted once

Both declarations must be captured as a visible sequence of steps that shows how much is left, allow a part-finished answer to be saved and resumed, and show everything back before submission. A draft must never count as a submission: until the person submits they remain outstanding, and the system must say which state they are in. On submission the declaration must be issued to the declarant as a PDF and remain readable afterwards.

Related party transactions

HLR-5

A transaction can only be raised against a declared related party

The system must restrict the counter-party of a new transaction to parties already on the Related Party Master, raise it in the name of the person submitting it, and require the material terms and supporting documents. Its pre-checks — counter-party on the master, value against the submitter's delegated authority — must be shown with their result rather than applied silently.

HLR-6

Every transaction shows where it is, who holds it, and how long they have held it

The system must give the requestor and the approver the same account of a transaction: its status, the person it is currently with named rather than described by position, the full history including each amendment and each decision with its remarks, and the time remaining before it escalates. An approver must be able to return it with remarks that the requestor sees. After thirty days without action it must escalate automatically and record the reason.

HLR-7

The governance decision is recorded with its evidence before release

The system must let the CCAO record a decision taken outside it — the deciding body, the meeting date, the members who abstained as conflicted, and the compliance conclusion — with the minutes attached, and must require that record before a transaction can be released to the register. The register must then hold every released transaction with its approvals, its decision and its dates, filterable and exportable for an auditor.

Reporting and data

HLR-8

A report is read as the document it will become

Every report must present as the paginated sheet it will be printed as, opening with the figures that answer the question it exists to answer. It must be printable, exportable as a server-generated PDF identical for every recipient, and exportable as a spreadsheet with a filterable header. Every one of those outputs must state the filters it was taken with — a report that does not say what it excluded cannot be distinguished from one that found nothing.

HLR-9

The compliance returns answer who was asked and who complied

The system must produce, for any period: who was required to declare and who has, per declaration type; a single person's declared interests in full; who holds an account and which modules each can reach; and share dealing over a date range. Each must be reproducible later for the same period — a return filed for a past quarter must still render as it did.

HLR-10

Legacy records move across without loss and without invention

The system must migrate the legacy users, declarations, related party master and transactions, with their original dates and their attached documents re-linked. It must be runnable without writing anything, repeatable without duplicating anything, and must not overwrite a record edited since. Where the legacy data has no equivalent for a field, the field must be left unset and reported rather than guessed — and the run must produce a reconciliation a reviewer can sign.
